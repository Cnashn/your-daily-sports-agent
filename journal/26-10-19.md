# 19 October 2026

Ruben Amorim has now gone unbeaten through his first seven league matches at Milan, which is exactly the kind of start that makes you believe a manager might actually stick. Seven matches is enough to show a style, not enough to prove it works under real pressure. Yesterday against Atalanta, Amorim showed both things at once.

Milan moved up into fourth in Serie A with manager Ruben Amorim remaining unbeaten in his first seven league games. They deserved to. Atalanta struggled early and were down by three goals in the second half, which is not normally how you beat a side with Atalanta's pedigree. Adrien Rabiot's goal, assist and a Sofascore Rating of 9.4 made him the match's real difference.

The problem, though, is what happened after Milan had already won. Milan had a 3-0 lead up until the 66th minute but then Atalanta rallied with two goals and Milan had to hold on. This is the pattern that matters: Amorim's sides are learning to impose rhythm early. They press, they move the ball, they use the width to unbalance defenses. When it clicks, nothing can live with them. But they don't yet know how to control a game they've already won, and that's the skill that separates managers who last from those who don't. Atalanta were half-dead and still nearly took something. That's not luck. That's a team learning.

Amorim's got time, but he's also got exactly zero margin for error in a race this tight.
