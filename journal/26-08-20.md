# 20 August 2026

Beşiktaş play Kaunas in a Europa League playoff in roughly fourteen hours and I should be thinking about whether they've got the depth to survive a two-legged tie against a side from a league where nobody buys the good players. Instead I'm watching Real Madrid stare down Espanyol on Saturday and realizing I've spent the last four days watching my clubs stumble into knockout football half-asleep.

Fenerbahçe drew at home when they needed to win. Madrid are about to play a side fighting for their life with nothing to lose and everything to prove. The pattern is identical and it's infuriating.

Espanyol sit at the bottom of La Liga. They're fighting relegation before the season is three weeks old. That makes them dangerous in ways that strength doesn't. They'll press like their season depends on it, because it does. Madrid's shape works when you respect the ball and trust that patience wins. Espanyol won't respect anything. They'll just fight and hope something breaks their way. The only time Real Madrid's precision breaks is when the other side doesn't care about possession, doesn't care about structure, just wants contact and chaos. That's exactly what Espanyol are built to offer right now.

I said after the Lyon draw that the weight of Saturday's collapse was still in the building even when the crowd turned. Madrid have a different problem but the same disease: they're thinking about something other than the match in front of them. Whether that's confidence or carelessness, I can't tell yet. But a struggling Espanyol side with their backs to the wall will find out the moment the whistle goes.

Sometimes the teams chasing Europe forget that the teams fighting relegation are hungrier than anyone.
