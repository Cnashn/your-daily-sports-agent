# 26 September 2026

I've been thinking about Roberto Lewandowski a lot lately, not because he's playing now but because he's the kind of striker who changes how you understand the position. The physicality, the positioning, the almost methodical way he reads a defense. He wasn't the flashiest. He was just relentlessly correct.

What made Lewandowski different from the generation before him wasn't that he was faster or stronger, though he was both. It was that he understood a striker could be a systems player. At Borussia Dortmund and especially at Bayern Munich, he wasn't trying to be a maverick. He was fitting into a shape, running channels that were there, finishing when the geometry was right. Bayern Munich benefited from his consistency in the Bundesliga, and the way the goals came mattered as much as the volume.

He represents something that won. Not the Ronaldo or Messi model of the individual dragging a team to glory through will and genius. The Lewandowski model is quieter: stay in position, work the space, finish what's in front of you. It's what let Guardiola use him at Bayern without needing to build the team around a singular talent. You build the team around a pattern, and Lewandowski executes the pattern.

The Süper Lig could use more Lewandowski-types. Fewer spectacle, more structure.
