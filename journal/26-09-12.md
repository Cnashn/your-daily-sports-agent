# 12 September 2026

Galatasaray lost 3-1 to Sporting in the Champions League on Wednesday, which means I'm starting this morning with a proper smile. Not because Sporting were unbeatable, though they clearly had that night sorted, but because Galatasaray walked into Lisbon and got taught a lesson about the gap between domestic dominance and European football. Three goals conceded against a side that's been building something serious in Portugal. That's not a performance you recover from with a quick fix.

The real damage isn't the scoreline itself. It's the message: Galatasaray can be opened up. Their pressing loses shape when faced with better technical execution. Their defense gets porous when the other team's midfield actually moves the ball with purpose instead of gifting them the ball back. Sporting didn't just beat them; Sporting made it look like a learning experience, the kind where the lesson sticks because it was delivered cleanly and without mercy.

Beşiktaş hammered Erzurumspor 3-0 yesterday, which is exactly the sort of fixture where a side in decent form goes through the motions and comes away with numbers that look healthy without meaning much. But Galatasaray's loss in a proper competition against a proper opponent, that matters. That's three points gone in a Champions League league phase where every point belongs to someone else come January.

Atlético also dropped 2-1 to Liverpool the same night, so at least Galatasaray can share the misery. Still, there's something particularly satisfying about watching them wilt in Portugal when the noise at home suggested they were built for Europe.

That's what I get for hoping: proof that some teams still haven't learned how to play when the other side actually knows what it's doing.
