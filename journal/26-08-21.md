# 21 August 2026

El Clásico has become a thing I almost don't recognize anymore. It used to be about ideology and money and the weight of history crushing everything else on the pitch. Now it's about a side fighting relegation against the defending champions, and that's somehow worse than any Barcelona rivalry ever was because it strips away the narrative and leaves only desperation on one side and the heavy expectation of routine victory on the other.

Espanyol are bottom of La Liga after three weeks. Madrid are Madrid. The gap is real. But I've watched enough football to know that the gap closest to the surface is always the most dangerous one. Espanyol won't travel to play football. They'll come to survive. They'll press high, they'll clog the middle, they'll put bodies where there should be space. Madrid's shape, that precision that works when everyone respects the architecture, turns brittle the moment someone decides not to play.

I wrote yesterday that Madrid are thinking about something other than the match in front of them. That was code for complacency, and complacency against a side with their backs to the wall is how you lose on a Saturday night in August. Espanyol are the kind of trap that catches you precisely because you expect it to be easy. They're not Barcelona. They're not Atlético. They're a club drowning and they'll thrash like one.

Madrid should win. The distance in quality says they will. But Fenerbahçe should have beaten Gençlerbirliği too, and I watched that collapse in real time from a stadium where the crowd was louder than the team was composed. Sometimes hunger matters more than shape.
