# 7 November 2026

Beşiktaş had it won in Glasgow on Thursday night. They went up twice at Celtic Park, controlled the second half when it mattered, and they still managed to throw it away in the final eight minutes. Junior Olaitan scored from the rebound after Sam Johnstone saved his penalty, and then Orkun Kökçü went up 2-1 with just five minutes left. That's not a half-chance or a scramble. That's a comfortable position. And then Celtic's substitutes did what Beşiktaş couldn't: they stayed composed and finished it.

Callum Osmand equalized almost immediately after coming on, and Camilo Durán got the winner with the very last action. Beşiktaş had their chances, their control of the match, and their two-goal lead. They just didn't have the mentality to close it out when a Scottish side was throwing numbers at them in desperation. That's the kind of loss that defines European campaigns, the kind that tells you whether a team actually wants to compete in this competition or just show up.

Beşiktaş cling to eighth place thanks to other results, which means they're hanging by a thread. They're not out yet, but they're also not in control of anything anymore. One bad night in Glasgow and suddenly the Europa League looks like a genuine problem instead of just a distraction from their domestic struggles.

When you score twice in Europe and still lose, the scoreline is almost beside the point.
