# 6 November 2026

Atlético Madrid had no answer at the Metropolitano on Tuesday night. Not bad luck, not fine margins, not an off day. Bayern simply rolled through them 2-0 and made it look inevitable from the moment they decided to turn up in the second half.

The first 45 minutes were goalless, which suggests Simeone managed to frustrate the visitors into something resembling a game. Then the second half arrived and Karl pounced on a defensive mistake after 54 minutes, and whatever small advantage Atlético had built evaporated. Atlético put up just 0.83 expected goals compared to Bayern's 1.77, which is not a close contest dressed up in scoreline language. That's a team that was outplayed in every way that mattered.

What makes it worse for Atlético is not the scoreline but the position it leaves them in. Four points after four matches in a 36-team league phase means they're sitting in the relegation zone of the new format, needing at minimum to reach the top eight. Bayern, meanwhile, already has eight points and breathing room. In competitions like this the gap between comfortable and drowning appears within days, not weeks.

Simeone's defensive discipline usually makes Atlético competitive even when they're outmatched. It didn't work here. Konrad Laimer doubled the lead in the 74th minute with a clinical finish after a brilliant Kane pass, and at that point I stopped waiting for Atlético to reply.

They're not getting out of this with their current trajectory. Neither are they improving from here in the next few weeks.
