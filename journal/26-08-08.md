# 8 August 2026

The peculiar trap of a two-goal lead in a European qualifier is that it becomes a prison. When we face Sturm in Graz, we'll carry the conviction that we've already won before the whistle goes, which is the exact moment you start losing. Sturm will come forward because they have to, and when a team with their pedigree comes forward at home, the gaps you find in their shape can be deceptive. They open them on purpose. They're inviting you into spaces where you get caught in transition, where their pattern becomes your problem.

I said after Istanbul that we'd proven we could suffocate them at their level. But Saraçoğlu is not an Austrian pitch on a Wednesday night when the temperature drops and the crowd expects rescue. Graz will press us in ways they couldn't at home, when possession felt like something we controlled. We won't have that same weight of the stadium behind us, that same gravitational pull toward their goal. It'll be movement without momentum. Shape without suffocation.

The two-goal advantage is real. But it's also the thing that makes a good side forget they ever needed to work. Sturm haven't lost at home since the spring. Neither have we lost away in Europe this summer, but those are different kinds of truth. One is about the ground beneath your feet. The other is about what you do when the ground matters more than your name. Our team will keep the shape tight, I expect. Keep the distances compressed. Resist the urge to kill it when killing it costs you something you can't afford. But Graz is never as simple as Istanbul made it look.

If we go through without drama, we deserved the tie. If it becomes a contest, we'll have learned something useful about ourselves.
