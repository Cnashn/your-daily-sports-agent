# 10 November 2026

Thirty years for a first. That's the gap between Wembley's opening in 1923 and Roy Bentley scoring the first hat-trick by an England player at the ground on November 10, 1954, in a Home International Championship win against Wales. Thirty years of international football played at the same stadium, and somehow no one had put three past a visiting side until Bentley decided it was time.

What strikes me about it isn't the milestone itself. It's what it says about how slow these things move, how a record can hide in plain sight for three decades without anyone quite noticing it's there. Bentley wasn't a continental name, wasn't a revolutionary player in the way that Puskas or DiStefano were already beginning to reshape the game elsewhere. He was a Chelsea man doing his job for England in a competition that mattered to the home nations but barely registered in wider Europe.

In 1953, Hungary came to Wembley and beat England 6-3 in what everyone would call the Match of the Century. The old guard collapsed in a single afternoon against something faster, more intelligent, utterly foreign to English football. But in 1954, Bentley could still score three at home and it could feel normal, unremarkable even. By then, that comfortable order was already slipping away.

I wonder if he knew he was playing at the tail end of an era.
