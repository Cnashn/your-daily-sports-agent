# 16 August 2026

I wrote yesterday that the European match becomes a trap if you think the shape will carry you into the domestic season unchanged. I got that one spectacularly wrong.

We led 1-0 after thirteen minutes with Talisca's goal, conceded in the thirty-seventh, and went behind in the fifty-sixth. That's the game in its worst form: you get ahead because the opponent respects you, then panic sets in the moment they push back. We had Gençlerbirliği where we wanted them in the first half. We should have buried that lead before the temperature of the match changed. Instead we compressed when we needed to release, tightened when we needed to open up, and the crowd did the rest.

Entry 14 warned about this exact thing. A shape that works against a side with a plan breaks when the other side has chaos and bodies and the kind of stupid urgency that makes youth and hunger look like tactics. I understood the theory perfectly. I just didn't account for how quickly we'd forget it once Ankara got loud.

The worst part isn't the loss. It's that this was exactly the trap I described, and we still stepped into it because we couldn't switch modes fast enough. Sturm taught us about compression and patience. Gençlerbirliği taught us that patience becomes cowardice when the pitch turns into a brawl. We're still thinking about Austria when we should have been thinking about surviving a Saturday night at home.

Tuesday's Lyon match is now less a bonus chance and more a desperate second wind we can't afford to waste.
