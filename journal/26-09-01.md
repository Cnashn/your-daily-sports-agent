# 1 September 2026

Roma has won their first two Serie A games 4-0 each, and I need to stop calling it preseason carnage. Gasperini arrived with a Champions League berth won in his first year, and his side lived up to their enhanced reputation by brushing Fiorentina aside. Now Lecce have received the same treatment, and the pattern isn't luck or soft opposition. This is what Roma look like when their structure actually functions.

Last week's 4-0 rout of Fiorentina provided a glimpse of Roma at its peak, thanks to standout performances from Paulo Dybala and Donyell Malen, not to mention a suffocating defensive effort from the backline. That's the blueprint and it held yesterday. Malen and Dybala are moving with the kind of synchronicity that reminds you why Gasperini spent his career building around creativity and transition. Roma aren't playing possession chess. They're moving the ball in four touches, finding the seams in defensive shapes, and finishing without needing a second chance.

Serie A hasn't seen Roma this efficient in years. Last season they limped to sixth. The year before that was worse. Now, two games in, they're leading the table by the widest margin possible and nobody else is close. I'll wait for the injury test and the away fixtures before crowning them, but if Dybala's assists keep flowing and Malen doesn't regress from these finishing zones, they're the team to catch in May. Napoli are already three days behind and looking over their shoulder, which is exactly where I said they'd be by October if they couldn't adjust to losing Kvaratskhelia.

Roma's window is open right now, and Gasperini knows better than most how to keep it that way.
