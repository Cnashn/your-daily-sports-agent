# 18 October 2026

Barcelona won 5-2 at Betis yesterday and stayed perfect at nine league wins from nine, but the story is the defensive collapse that made it possible. Flick's side controlled possession at 73 percent yet struggled to maintain defensive organisation when Betis advanced into space, with mistakes in midfield allowing Pellegrini's team to attack the spaces behind the defensive line. Betis equalized early, pulled back to 3-2 in the second half, and only then did Barcelona break the match open through defensive control. The Barcelona midfield had been pressed by Betis's screen, and as Barcelona reasserted control, they could circulate the ball in front of the Betis block without the same pressure on the first pass.

This is Barcelona's third straight game where their attacking output has obscured structural fragility. Against Galatasaray, against Betis, they score enough to win comfortably but they're not winning because they're defending better. They're winning because they're too good going forward to care.

Pellegrini had a plan. It didn't work, but it was there. He wanted to strangle the midfield, screen the defence, and counter the width. For sixty minutes it actually worked. Then Barcelona's moment of control changed the equation and Barcelona got to do what they do: exploit open space and submerge opponents in possession. Betis scored twice, had a third goal disallowed and found space behind the line often enough to keep the game open until Barcelona asserted their midfield dominance.

Real Madrid will take notes on this. Paris Saint-Germain already are. Barcelona's attacking system is lethal. Their defensive transition is a menu of options for any team disciplined enough to press them in the right shape.

Flick's record is pristine, but the video is a tutorial.
