# 5 September 2026

I got this entirely wrong, and now I have to sit with it. Madrid travelled to Betis and didn't just lose, they got caught out exactly the way I said wouldn't happen if they played properly. Kylian Mbappé missed a stoppage-time penalty as Real Madrid suffered their first defeat since the return of coach José Mourinho, with a 1-0 loss at Real Betis. A predicted comfortable win instead became a humbling.

The problem wasn't Betis doing anything revolutionary. Betis stunned Madrid in the 81st minute when a ball was knocked in that squirmed through a congested box, and from there Betis just had to survive. They did. Betis goalkeeper Álvaro Valles smothered Mbappé's spot kick in the fourth minute of added time, and the whole narrative flipped.

I said if Madrid got sloppy with possession they'd lose, if they chased Betis's transitions they'd be in trouble. Both happened. Madrid had nearly all the ball, dominated the first half, and still couldn't create anything decisive until desperation set in. That's not Betis suddenly learning to stifle quality. That's Madrid playing without menace, moving the ball without finding angles, and relying on individual brilliance to break a disciplined press instead of actually breaking it through structure. When that failed, Betis nicked the opening that was always going to come, and fortune favored the home side.

Mourinho's first defeat back stings more because it shows the fragility I glimpsed coming in wasn't a warning. It was a prediction. A match kicks off at 1:00 PM Eastern in about seven hours, and this is exactly the temperature reset I needed to feel, except it's not my team it happened to.

Madrid face Internazionale on Tuesday, and you don't recover from nights like Seville by playing one of the continent's sharpest sides in a new Champions League league phase.
