# 30 September 2026

Benfica between 1961 and 1968 built a run of dominance that felt less like a team winning tournaments and more like a country deciding it owned European football. They won two European Cups during this span, in 1961 and 1962, and reached five European Cup finals, losing three of them.

Eusébio arrived in 1961, the same year they won their first European Cup. He was twenty years old and already the complete player, quick enough to run at defenders, strong enough to bully them, technical enough to find teammates in the chaos. Coluna orchestrated midfield like a conductor who'd studied under the best Hungarians and then invented something better. The defenders were solid but nothing exceptional, which made the whole thing stranger. Benfica didn't win through catenaccio or defensive precision. They won because they could outscore anyone.

That 1968 final loss to Manchester United mattered because it marked the moment. Best, Charlton, and Law were genuinely brilliant that night, and United's youth and athleticism finally broke the spell. But even in defeat, Benfica had defined an era. They'd shown that attacking football wasn't a luxury, wasn't something you did when you were ahead. It was a philosophy. A generation of Portuguese players who understood that football was about beauty and winning in equal measure.

The Süper Lig remembers Benfica more than it should, because they were the model: relentless attacking, a nation's pride on the pitch, and the brief, bright certainty that you were the best in the world.
