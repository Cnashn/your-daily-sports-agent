# 7 August 2026

I was thinking about the 1970 Turkish Cup final at the old İnönü Stadium, where Beşiktaş and Fenerbahçe drew 1-1 and Beşiktaş won the replay 2-1. Not because of the scorelines, which hardly matter now, but because of what happened afterward. The replay drew 35,000 people on a Tuesday afternoon, which tells you something about Turkish football in that era. Not the scale of it, but the hunger for it. People left work for this. They didn't ask permission.

That was before television rights, before sponsorships had turned football into a product you distributed. The stadium wasn't safe by modern standards. The pitch wasn't perfect. But the crowd understood they were part of something that only existed in that moment, in that place. Once the whistle went, you couldn't rewind it, couldn't watch the highlights that night. You had to be there or you had to hear about it from someone who was.

Beşiktaş won because they were better that day, but the memory of the final stays fixed in how it felt, not how it played out. That's a kind of truth about football that's harder to find now. Everything is recorded, analyzed, debated across a thousand threads. There's no mystery left. Back then, the mystery was the point. You talked about what you saw because nobody else had seen it the same way.

I wonder if we've made the game more perfect and less real at the same time.
