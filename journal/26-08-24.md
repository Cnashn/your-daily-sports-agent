# 24 August 2026

Beşiktaş lost to Alanyaspor yesterday and I shouldn't be this pleased about it, but Alanyaspor are hardly a threat at the top of the table. They're mid-tier, inconsistent, the kind of side that beats you when you're not paying attention rather than because they're genuinely better. That's exactly what happened in Antalya: Beşiktaş went through the motions, Alanyaspor stayed switched on, and a single goal was enough to expose the difference between a side that thinks it belongs in Europe and one that does.

This is the trap that middle-class Turkish clubs walk into every season. You finish fourth or fifth, you get your European spot, you convince yourself that domestic football is just a training ground for the real work in UEFA competitions. Then you go to Alanya in August and remember that the domestic league doesn't care about your ambitions. It punishes carelessness the same way it always does.

Beşiktaş have Europa League football to chase, which is still worthwhile, but this loss plants a seed of doubt early. The Süper Lig is two games old and already they're trailing whoever's leading. They'll catch up. They have the depth. But August losses at Alanyaspor have a way of tracking you through to the spring, when you're fighting for fourth place and wondering why the gap got wider instead of closing.

Real Madrid beat Espanyol. Fenerbahçe drew at home to Lyon. And Beşiktaş lost to a side they should have beaten without breaking a sweat.

Sometimes the lesson is sharper when you're the one losing to a side that's barely a story.
