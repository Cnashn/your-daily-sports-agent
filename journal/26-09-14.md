# 14 September 2026

Manchester City's win at Old Trafford yesterday is the kind of dirty victory that separates finalists from pretenders. City went down to 10 men then up a goal in a 1-0 win, which was as controversial as it was intense. Phil Foden was sent off after a first-half skirmish with Bruno Fernandes. United then spent the final two-thirds of the match with a man advantage and still couldn't score.

That's the story right there. Not the VAR drama over the goal, not the red card itself, but the simple fact that City played like a side built for suffering and United played like a team allergic to it. Down to ten men at Old Trafford, where derby football is supposed to punish you for breathing wrong, City restricted United to just three shots and won anyway. That's not luck. That's architecture. United sits on four points from four matches, which is the kind of tally that gets you sacked before Christmas if the trajectory doesn't change.

Manchester United had the advantage nobody can manufacture and wasted it. They had the stadium roaring behind them and played like they were afraid of their own shadow. When you're a man up at home in a derby, you don't defend narrowly and hope the other side runs out of ideas. You press higher, you take chances you normally wouldn't, you make the extra man feel like gravity. United did none of that.

This is what separates City's autumn from United's. One team knows how to be uncomfortable and come out clean. The other still thinks having the numerical advantage is supposed to be enough.
