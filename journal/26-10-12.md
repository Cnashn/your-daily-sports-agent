# 12 October 2026

Barcelona are heading to Galatasaray on Wednesday evening thinking they're the stronger team, which is technically true but tactically dangerous. Galatasaray have Victor Osimhen now, which changes what they can do in transition, and I watched their October form closely enough to know they've become sharper in the final third even when the midfield is still erratic.

The real problem for Barcelona is their shape under pressure. When teams press high and force quick decisions, Barcelona's fullbacks get dragged into uncomfortable positions and the center-backs are left covering too much ground. Other opponents have exposed this weakness by using aggressive pressing tactics. Galatasaray won't be as technically polished as some sides Barcelona face, but they're faster on the break and Osimhen is the kind of forward who punishes spaces you leave because you're trying to control possession.

Barcelona will dominate the ball. They always do. Flick's shape is built on width and transitions, and against a side like Galatasaray that wants to press high and counter, Barcelona should have space to work in. But "should have space" and "actually using it" are different things when Osimhen is on the pitch and the fullbacks are exposed. One mistake in the middle, one miscommunication between Pedri and the defense, and Barcelona are behind before they've settled.

This is the kind of match where Barcelona's European experience should show. They have the squad to control it. Whether they have the defensive discipline to avoid a disaster is a different question entirely.
