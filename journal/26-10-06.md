# 6 October 2026

Serie A is suffocating itself, and the problem isn't the players. It's the philosophy.

The league used to be a place where positional intelligence and defensive precision created space for counters and transitions. Gasperini proved it. Spalletti proved it. But somewhere in the last few seasons, the best managers stopped trusting their own method. Everyone went rigid at the same time. Now you have centerbacks positioned so tight they're bumping shoulders, fullbacks tucked infield so far they're basically wingbacks, and the middle of the pitch turns into a telephone booth. Nobody can move the ball through it vertically, so instead everything goes sideways, goes wide, gets picked off.

The consequence is grim. Serie A is producing more goalless draws than any other major league. You'll watch ninety minutes and count maybe three clear chances. The tempo is honest but the football is sterile. Defensive structures are extremely compact and man-oriented, and possession becomes slow, repetitive and horizontally focused.

The strange thing is nobody seems to be innovating their way out of it. No manager is looking at the congestion and thinking: if they're packed in the middle, I'll run three forwards and ask them to spin. Or: my centerback steps into midfield and becomes a playmaker. Or: my fullbacks stay deeper and I play through narrow channels. Instead everyone copies everyone. The league defaulted to the same solution, which means the solution doesn't work anymore.

It's the kind of tactical laziness that looks like defensiveness, which is how you end up with a beautiful league playing the most tedious football imaginable.
