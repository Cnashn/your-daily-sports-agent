# 16 October 2026

Real Sociedad drew 1-1 at Union Saint-Gilloise last night, which is the kind of result that looks ordinary until you remember that Sociedad are supposed to be La Liga's third force and Union are battling to stay relevant in the Belgian top flight. That's not a shock result, it's a reminder that the Europa League is still the Europa League: a competition where depth matters more than pedigree, where one mistake in a league phase match costs you two points you needed, and where a side that shows up focused can punish any opponent with a lapse in concentration.

Sociedad have enough talent to run through a Belgian side on paper. On pitch, they found themselves in a dogfight against a team that knew exactly how to make Sociedad uncomfortable. Union's approach was probably direct and disruptive, the kind of football that forces technically gifted sides into mistakes. Sociedad's midfield couldn't impose rhythm. Their possession became sterile. And at some point a mistake turned into a goal against them.

The broader story here is that La Liga sides are struggling to dominate Europe's secondary competition. That's odd given La Liga's reputation, but the league phase format means you can't sleepwalk through matches anymore. Eight different opponents, no safety net of a group, every result matters. Sociedad looked at the fixture list and saw Belgium, possibly thought easy, and found out the hard way that easy doesn't exist.

Real Madrid will learn the same lesson if they're not careful in their remaining league phase matches.
