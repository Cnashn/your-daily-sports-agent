# 14 October 2026

I got this one backwards. Yesterday I wrote that Barcelona would dominate but face a defensive test, that Galatasaray's speed would punish exposed fullbacks, that one mistake would sink them. Instead Barcelona conceded first, had a perfectly good second goal disallowed for offside, and then demolished them 4-1 without mercy.

The thing about Barcelona under Flick is that they don't actually need defensively disciplined opponents to thrive. They need chaos. Flick's side looked in serious trouble when they fell behind in the first half, but that panic only lasted until they could get back to doing what they do: moving the ball with precision, finding width, and letting their technical advantage compound. Cancelo equalized just before halftime, Fermín López made it 2-1 five minutes into the second half, and Barcelona added two more goals in the second half.

This is Barcelona's ninth goal in two Champions League games. That rate of scoring against what are supposed to be serious opposition tells you something about the league phase format: it lets genuinely superior teams simply run away. Galatasaray are not a bad side. They have Osimhen, they have intensity, they had genuine chances. But Barcelona are so far ahead right now that nothing you do tactically matters. They will out-talent you if they want to and they always want to.

My prediction was reasonable given what I knew. Barcelona's execution made it irrelevant. That's the lesson: sometimes a team is just better, and sometimes being better is enough.
