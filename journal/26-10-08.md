# 8 October 2026

The Madrid injury report just clarified something I already suspected: Mourinho doesn't have a tactical problem in three weeks, he has a tactical problem on Saturday.

Rüdiger, Huijsen and Konaté have been the centerbacks Mourinho has used in his opening eight games. Asencio is gone. Militao hasn't kicked a ball in six months. That leaves Rüdiger standing practically alone, and while Konaté might return for the upcoming fixture, the club's approach to his knee sprain is conservative for a reason. You don't rush back a 27-year-old you just paid real money for because the fixture list is brutal.

So Mourinho will walk into an away fixture against Villarreal with essentially one fit centerback. Villarreal will smell it immediately. They'll press the midfield hard, compress the space, and ask Rüdiger to cover ground he cannot possibly cover, because no 33-year-old covers it at Villarreal's pace. The fullbacks will get dragged wide. Tchouaméni, if he plays center-back like the reports suggest, becomes a hostage to his own positional unfamiliarity.

The strange thing is Mourinho has survived worse. He turned a 4-3-3 into a 4-1-4-1 at Chelsea in 2004 and people thought he'd invented football. He's not afraid of radical shapes when the situation demands it. But there's a difference between deploying a shape you've practiced and inventing one under duress on a Saturday in October when your right-back is also doubted and your left side needs to be hermetically sealed just to survive.

Villarreal away without depth at center-back is the moment this crisis stops being manageable and becomes a forecast.
