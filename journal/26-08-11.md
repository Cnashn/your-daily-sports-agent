# 11 August 2026

Three weeks left in the transfer window and everyone's lying. The clubs saying they're satisfied with their squads are the ones still making calls at midnight. The managers claiming depth is good enough know it isn't. The boards insisting they're done are refreshing their email. Late August is when football lives in the space between what's been built and what should have been, and every team pretends the gap doesn't exist until it opens up in September.

Preseason has ground toward its close. The friendlies mean nothing now, even the ones that mattered two weeks ago. Sturm finished their preparation matches and now we show up in Graz tonight, which tells you something about how desperate qualifying rounds are: even the matches designed to prepare you for real football are barely real. One eye on the shape, one eye on whether someone picked up a knock. By the time the league actually starts, half the squads won't look like they were built to play it.

The Turkish season kicks off Friday. Galatasaray still haven't convinced me they learned anything from Villarreal. Their shape opens up when they're pressed, their midfield stops thinking when bodies come forward, their finishing flirts with panic. But the domestic campaign is a different animal. The crowd matters. The rhythm matters. Against league opposition they'll be sharp again, which is exactly why August tells you nothing about what happens when European football demands more. They'll go through the season looking like champions at home and pretenders in Europe, and they'll never quite understand why.

Tonight we prove whether Istanbul changed us or just got lucky in August. The two-goal lead should carry us through. If it doesn't, if Sturm find something in Graz that we couldn't suffocate, then the whole shape I thought we'd built was an illusion.

Three weeks to fix everything nobody believed was broken.
