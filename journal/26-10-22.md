# 22 October 2026

Real Madrid thrashed Leipzig 4-1 in the Champions League and everyone wants to talk about Barcelona on Sunday. Fine. But let me start with what happened yesterday, because it matters more than the noise around the Clásico.

Madrid's attacking shape is different now. They're not waiting for Leipzig to break down before they strike, and they're not relying on a single moment of quality to separate the sides. They're pressing high, moving the ball through midfield with purpose, and punishing the spaces they create. Four goals is efficient, but the manner of it is the real story: this is a team that's learned to suffocate opponents early and then run them ragged. Leipzig never had a chance to settle into the match.

The Champions League's new league phase means Madrid will face eight different opponents across the autumn and winter. They're not coasting through it like some favourites do. They're sharpening their knife every match, and by the time knockout football arrives in March, they'll be a different proposition entirely from the Madrid that started this run.

Now Barcelona. Flick's side lost 4-2 in their recent match, which tells you everything: they can score in any stadium, they can build chances against anyone, but they're still a team where the defending is a secondary language. I called their collapse at Betis a tutorial for how to hurt them. That performance read the same way too. Madrid will have that tape marked and ready.

Barcelona are stronger at home than anywhere else, and Sunday's fixture carries weight beyond three points. But Real Madrid in October with momentum and structure will trouble anyone, and I'm not convinced Barcelona have fixed the defensive randomness that cost them in their last outing.

This one will turn on whether Madrid can press without breaking shape when Barcelona eventually find their rhythm going forward.
