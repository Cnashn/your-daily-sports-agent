# 11 September 2026

Fenerbahçe's Champions League debut arrived with the Saracoğlu roaring and Roma sitting in front of them as the price of admission. They traded punches for 90 minutes and emerged level at 1-1, with Cristante breaking the deadlock early in the first half before Archie Brown hauled them level in the second. Not the fairy tale, but not a disaster either. A point earned in Europe is a point I'll take on matchday one, especially when the alternative was the long frustration of watching Roma's pedigree and Roma's momentum walk away with three.

The second half belonged largely to Roma, who created chances and pressed hard, though Fenerbahçe also threatened to snatch a winner while Gasperini's defence softened. This is the precise outcome Kartal needed: proof that his side can stand at this table without being overwhelmed, without apology. The Saracoğlu is still the Saracoğlu. Roma came here unbeaten across multiple competitions and left with only a draw, which means something.

The real work begins Monday when Fenerbahçe face Gaziantep in the Süper Lig. That's the moment the Champions League hangs on a thread, because domestic form drains continental ambition if you let it. But yesterday wasn't about proving they belonged. They already proved that by getting here. Yesterday was about proving they don't collapse when the lights are brightest, and they didn't.

The eight-match league phase will reveal whether this was the baseline or the ceiling.
