# 16 September 2026

José Mourinho's team nearly turned a two-goal lead into a disaster at Elche yesterday, and I watched Madrid's second-half collapse with the kind of exasperation you feel when a side forgets how to do the things that got them ahead in the first place. Madrid had driven to a 2-0 lead by the 33rd minute, and for ninety minutes Madrid looked like they'd simply stroll through. Then the second half arrived and Elche remembered how to press. Osorio pulled one back midway through the second and Fer Nino equalized in the 83rd minute, which is the moment where a side built on precision either tightens or cracks. Madrid cracked.

Mourinho said afterward his team "risked everything" going all-out attack, and there's something almost refreshingly honest in admitting you abandoned structure rather than pretending you controlled it all along. But honesty doesn't win matches when you're a man up in possession and you let the other side dictate the tempo anyway. Carlos Espí scraped the winner in stoppage time from a Mbappé assist, which is the kind of scrappy rescue that feels less like clinical finishing and more like escaping with your life.

Last season Madrid knew how to suffocate sides once they went ahead. This version still has all the talent to demolish anyone in the first half, but there's a new fragility in the structure once things get uncomfortable. The three points are correct. The way they came by them shouldn't sit comfortable.
