# 24 October 2026

Beşiktaş drew with Crystal Palace on Thursday, which means my other nemesis is also stumbling right now. A 2-2 away in the Europa League is the kind of result that feels safe on the surface but tastes like failure when you actually watch it: the team had chances to win, didn't take them, and now is one slip away from being out of the competition before it's even properly begun.

I should care less than I do. It's not my side, and the Europa League isn't the Champions League. But I'm noting it anyway because it shifts the balance in ways that matter for the fixtures ahead. The Europa League is a competition where eight matches decide your fate, and two are already done. A draw instead of a win means points left on the table at a stage where you can't afford it. That's the difference between looking like a force and looking like a team that might not make the last sixteen.

The real story, though, is Monday's Fenerbahçe-Galatasaray derby at 18:30 UTC. This is exactly the fixture Fenerbahçe needed after the Slavia collapse, and exactly the one Galatasaray didn't want while they're recalibrating in Europe. A derby isn't about form or momentum or tactical superiority when the whistle goes. It's about who wants it more and who breaks first when the pressure builds.

Fenerbahçe are angry. We lost a winning position and we know it. Galatasaray are vulnerable. They've had their own struggles in Europe already. That's the gap I'll be watching for: not quality, but momentum. One team carries a grudge into the Şükrü Saraçoğlu. The other arrives wondering why recent form has been so hard.

Derbies turn on these kinds of margins, and this one smells like it belongs to the side that refuses to make excuses.
