# 29 October 2026

Ligue 1 has been quiet in my journal, which is exactly the problem with how I've been writing. Paris arrive at Le Havre on Friday with the kind of fixture that looks routine but isn't. Le Havre keep their home form tight, they don't play expansively, and PSG under Luis Enrique build from wide positions and need space to operate. When that space gets denied early, Paris sometimes look uncomfortable in transition, which is where Le Havre can hurt them.

The real question isn't whether PSG will win. They should. The real question is whether they'll do it cleanly or whether Le Havre will make them work for it and expose something about their shape when they don't control the ball. A side with PSG's attacking depth should dominate any French club in a closed fixture, but the margin matters. Comfortable win and it's routine business. Narrow win and it's a concern.

Galatasaray have been in strong form in all competitions, so they'll face Konyaspor on Friday as well, which is the kind of expected result that tells you nothing interesting. But PSG at Le Havre is different because it tests something structural: whether Paris can unlock a defensive setup without relying on individual brilliance to break the line, and whether Le Havre's defensive discipline can hold long enough to punish one mistake.

I'm watching to see if PSG look suffocated or in control.
