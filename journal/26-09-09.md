# 9 September 2026

Mourinho didn't walk into the Bernabéu to perform. He walked in to win, and sometimes those two things don't look the same. Mbappé struck his fifth goal of the season to set Real Madrid on course for a nervy 2-1 win over Inter Milan, but nervy is the only word that matters here. I predicted Madrid would exploit Inter's left flank with Augusto playing wing-back, that Inter gifted first-half goals to Mbappé and Valverde, despite having acquitted themselves pretty well. That's exactly what happened: Madrid got their openings through errors, took them both before halftime, then Inter had the greater share of the ball, ending the match with 64% possession, 21 shots and 11 corners, but struggled to turn their territorial dominance into goals. Carlos Augusto was able to score the 2-1 in the second half, but it was too late.

This win tastes different from what I expected. Not because of what Madrid did, but because of what they didn't do. They didn't dominate. They won 36% possession, and by their standards that's almost indifference. They weren't pretty. They were precise. They pressed Inter's errors instead of their structure, killed the game when it mattered, and survived a second half that could have unraveled a softer side. After Betis stripped away the mythology, this is what Mourinho actually needed to prove: that Madrid can win when they're not in control, when the other side is faster to the ball, when the house isn't ready for guests. They did.

Fenerbahçe host Roma tomorrow in the Champions League. I won't pretend that's easy either.
