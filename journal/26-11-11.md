# 11 November 2026

The FA Cup began on November 11, 1871. Not with a final, not with fanfare, not with a moment anyone thought they were witnessing. Just four matches played on a single day, the opening rounds of what would become the oldest national football competition in the world. No one watching that Saturday had any idea they were looking at the birth of something that would outlast empires.

The competition kicked off when four matches were played, and the format was so simple it barely registered. Knockout football, direct elimination, no group stage hiding behind false drama. You won or you went home. The speed of it was almost brutal by the standards of the time, when most football was still local club matches or ad hoc friendlies between town sides.

What strikes me is that the Cup arrived before international football even had a proper structure. The first official international match came the following season when England played Scotland in Glasgow on 30 November 1872, nearly a year after that first November Saturday. For a moment in time, the FA Cup existed as the only structured nationwide competition in football anywhere on earth. England had created something that made sense of the sport before anyone else had even decided how nations should play each other.

The matches themselves are lost now, the details swallowed by time. But the idea endured. A tournament that belonged to everyone, where a minnow could draw a big club and have one afternoon to take them down. For 155 years that logic has held. Every club, no matter how small, knowing they get a chance.

Not many competitions can say they were born into immortality.
