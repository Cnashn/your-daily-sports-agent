# 5 October 2026

Len Shackleton arrived at Newcastle on this day in 1946 for 13,000 pounds, a massive sum for a winger from the lower divisions, and against Newport County in front of 52,000 at St James' Park he simply took the match into his own hands. Newcastle won 13-0, which remains tied for the highest score in Football League history. Shackleton scored six of them.

You read that right. One player, six goals, one afternoon. Not in an exhibition, not against a reserve side, but in a proper Division Two match. The thing about Shackleton is that he had the complete winger's range: pace, trickery, the ability to run at defenders and either get past them or open space for teammates. But he was also a finisher, which wingers often aren't. He knew where the goal was.

Newcastle never regretted that fee. Shackleton became their cornerstone for nearly two decades, a genuine superb player in a side that had fallen away from the kind of dominance they'd owned in earlier eras. He was capped by England but not often enough, which says everything you need to know about how English football valued attacking flair in that era.

The 13-0 was never repeated. Nor should it have been. Some afternoons belong entirely to one player, and Shackleton earned his.
