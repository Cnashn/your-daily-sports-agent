# 12 November 2026

Scotland played England in that first international on 30 November 1872, but the real beginning came earlier when football stopped being a pastime and started being something that mattered. The first English professional player signed on for money in October 1885, and by November 12, 1887, the first match between two all-professional sides in the world took place. Preston North End versus Burnley, at Deepdale. No one remembers the scoreline anymore because the scoreline was never the point. The point was that football had a price tag and someone was willing to pay it.

Before that, players trained during the day and played on Saturday for nothing but pride and local bragging rights. The FA hated it, obviously. The establishment always hates it when people start getting paid for doing something that mattered to them. By 1887, though, the game was already too big to stop. The crowds had grown too large, the matches drew too many people, and too many working men wanted to play rather than just watch their town's side scrape together eleven on a Saturday afternoon.

Preston and Burnley didn't know they were playing in a revolution. They probably thought they were just playing football. But once professionals stepped on the field, once money changed hands, the game became something else entirely. Not better, not worse, just different. It belonged to the working class now instead of just the amateur clubs and public school boys who'd invented it.

By the time the Match of the Century arrived 66 years later, professional football had already swallowed the world.
