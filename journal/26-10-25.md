# 25 October 2026

Chelsea getting dismantled 2-0 at home by Tottenham matters more than the scoreline suggests. Conte's side has barely played in weeks, and when they finally returned, they looked sharp enough to expose every structural weakness Chelsea had been hiding behind fixture congestion. That's not a bad result. That's a statement: Tottenham are building something real, and Chelsea are still searching for basic defensive organization.

The Premier League's Saturday fixtures showed exactly what separates the sides right now. Arsenal's 2-0 over Everton was routine domination, the kind of performance you expect from a top team at home. Aston Villa's 2-1 over Manchester City was far more interesting: Villa pressed City early, denied them space in midfield, and punished the moments City looked loose. That's not a fluke. That's a side understanding that City can be troubled if you don't give them time to settle, and having the tactical discipline to execute it.

Arsenal are grinding forward with efficiency. Villa have genuine pace and intensity. Tottenham are building cohesion under Conte's system. And Chelsea, despite their spending and pedigree, are still the side most likely to lose focus and concede cheap goals at home. That gap matters more in a compressed fixture list than it does in isolation.

The Premier League's depth is real this season, but it's also revealing: the top six have separated into two tiers already, and it's October.

Liverpool and Brighton kick off in three hours, and I'm curious whether Brighton's underlying solidity can trouble a Liverpool side that's been playing well but hasn't yet faced a team that knows how to make them uncomfortable.
