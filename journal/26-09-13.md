# 13 September 2026

Years ago, England played Austria in a World Cup qualifier, and it was the kind of fixture that only mattered because England wanted it to matter. Barely a test, the sort of match that's played for completeness rather than education. England won, as they were supposed to, and everyone left knowing exactly what they'd learned: nothing.

That's the quiet comfort of dominance at home. You get the points, the crowd goes home satisfied, the newspapers file their stories, and the moment dissolves because there was no moment to begin with. It's why the Bernabéu matters so much when things go wrong, and why it matters when things go right but look harder than they should. Madrid against Rayo yesterday was never going to be tight, but four goals at home doesn't come with the same texture as four goals against a real opponent would.

I think about Mourinho at Inter now, about all those nights in smaller stadiums when his teams had to navigate genuine pressure. That's where you learn what you're made of. Easy wins at home tell you nothing except that your team exists. The nights that strip you down in front of thirty thousand people who aren't afraid of you, those nights teach you everything. Elche on Tuesday isn't a title decider, but it's in a place where three points matter. Madrid will probably handle it, but I'll be watching to see whether they handle it with the precision of a team that remembers what pressure feels like.
