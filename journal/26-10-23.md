# 23 October 2026

Union Saint-Gilloise and NEC Nijmegen arrived at Europa League matches yesterday with modest expectations and left with statement victories. Union dismantled Hapoel Be'er Sheva 4-0 at home, a scoreline that speaks to complete control. Nijmegen's 4-2 win at Dinamo Zagreb is the more interesting result because it came away from home, against a side with European pedigree, and because winning 4-2 in the Balkans means you imposed your football rather than merely surviving a local storm.

These aren't footnotes. This is the second round of league phase matches in the Europa League, which means the table is tightening and the gap between a team that can build momentum and a team already adrift is growing visible. Ferencváros beat Torreense 4-1 with similar authority. AZ Alkmaar destroyed Ararat 4-0 on the road. This is a tier of side that has either grasped the rhythm of European football in this new format or hasn't, and the ones that have are already pulling clear.

Lech Poznań drew 1-1 with Sunderland, which tells you both sides have defensive shape but limited attacking incision. That's a different problem: not domination, but stalemate. Sunderland will be satisfied. Lech less so, especially if they're chasing something in their own league.

The Europa League's league phase favors sides that can sustain intensity across multiple matches and multiple oppositions without the breathing room a group stage used to offer. You get eight matches, you get paid in points or you don't, and there's no second life waiting. Union, Nijmegen and Ferencváros are showing they understand this. The others are learning it the hard way.

Within three weeks, the real contenders will be obvious.
